# 0063. The panel's domain, set from the panel

- Status: Proposed. Amends [ADR 0042](0042-the-installs-settings.md), where it says how the panel is served is only `dockwell run`'s. Amended 2026-11-16 for a panel behind a tunnel or a proxy that ends TLS (#734), below.
- Date: 2026-11-10

## Context

A new install answers on loopback alone, through an SSH tunnel ([ADR 0011](0011-claim-and-sign-in.md)). To reach it over HTTPS on a domain, Settings → General says, in a list of steps: point the domain's DNS at the server, then `sudo systemctl edit dockwell` to add `Environment=DOCKWELL_DOMAIN=…` (or `/etc/conf.d/dockwell` with OpenRC, or `--domain` by hand), restart, check that ports 80 and 443 reach the server, then run `dockwell recover` over SSH and open its link on the new domain to make a passkey there. The claim page says much of the same under the panel's address.

ADR 0042 chose words over a field because the domain is read as `dockwell run` starts, so a field "would only take effect after a restart nobody asked for". Issue #426 holds every screen to "the interface does the work": a sentence that tells a person to do something by hand is a missing feature. 1Panel sets its panel's domain, port and HTTPS from its own settings.

What the domain shapes is all read at start: the relying party ID passkeys are made for, so a passkey belongs to the address it was made on ([ADR 0011](0011-claim-and-sign-in.md)); the HTTPS and HTTP listeners, or the hand-over to the server's Caddy when it has routes ([ADR 0024](0024-the-caddy-ingress-driver.md)); the certificate; the hosts every request is checked against; the claim link; and the name no route may take (`refuseClaimedDomain`, which stops `dockwell run` when a route already serves the name). Making each of those follow a change while the process runs would touch most of the control plane's start-up for a change made once or twice in an install's life.

Two moves need designing, not one: from loopback alone to a domain, where the person comes through the SSH tunnel and every passkey is the tunnel's; and from one domain to another, where people sign in on the first with its passkeys, most have no SSH, and the first stops answering when the second starts.

## Decision

**The panel's domain is a setting a person sets from Settings → General,** kept in the store as the install's name and time zone are, with an activity event that says what it replaced. It isn't managed state: it changes how the panel is reached, not what runs on a server (ADR 0042's exception, widened).

**Where `dockwell run` was given one, that one wins.** `--domain` or `DOCKWELL_DOMAIN` is read first, as today; the panel then shows the domain read-only, with where it's set. Without either, `dockwell run` reads the setting as it starts. Nothing an operator set on the command line or in the service's environment is overridden from the panel.

**Setting it is a restart the person asks for.** Saving says what will happen ("Dockwell restarts to serve panel.example.com. Apps and websites keep running.") and, once confirmed, the control plane shuts down cleanly and starts again from the same binary with the same arguments and environment: it re-executes itself, so the same happens under systemd, OpenRC and a `dockwell run` started by hand. The agent in the same process reconnects as it does after an upgrade. The objection in ADR 0042 was to a restart nobody asked for; this one is asked for with a button.

- **The answer goes out first.** The restart begins once the response to `SetPanelDomain` is written and flushed, so the panel knows the save took before its connection drops, and waits for the control plane to answer again.
- **It never cuts a step.** Saving is refused while a change is applying ("A change is applying. Save once it ends."), so the restart doesn't stop one partway.

**It's checked before it's saved, as the person types.** `SettingsService.CheckPanelDomain` answers, for an admin, as it has the control plane resolve the names typed:
- whether it's a name the panel can take: the rules `--domain` has, and no route serves it or a name under it (as `refuseClaimedDomain` refuses at start);
- what its A and AAAA records are, and whether they lead to this server's public addresses ([ADR 0034](0034-a-servers-public-address.md)), shown with the DNS record the websites track builds for #426 ("DNS doesn't point here yet · Check again");
- whether something other than Dockwell or its Caddy holds ports 80 or 443 on the control server, which would stop a certificate.

The save is refused while the name is invalid or taken by a route. DNS that doesn't lead here yet is a warning with the record to make, not a refusal: a person may set DNS and the domain in either order, and a certificate is tried again as it is today.

**`SettingsService.SetPanelDomain` sets or clears it.** It needs the owner, a signed-in session and a fresh passkey (a step-up, `STEP_UP_ACTION_SET_PANEL_DOMAIN`): the domain decides where everyone signs in. For an owner who chooses to be asked less often, the session stands in for the passkey ([ADR 0075](0075-how-often-a-passkey-is-asked-for-again.md)). Owner is a check in the handler, as for the install's other owner-only acts, since the roles table's `min_role` stops at admin. It's refused when `dockwell run` was given a domain. Clearing it goes back to loopback alone, except during a move, which it ends back on the old domain (below).

**From the moment it's saved, no route can take it.** The domain waiting for the restart is kept beside the one in use, and during a move so is the old one until its grace period ends; a route's plan is refused for any of them, or a name under one, as for the panel's domain today. So nothing can claim it between the save and the restart.

**The person who set it is handed the way in.** After the restart, the person's passkeys for the old address don't sign in on the new one. The panel waits for the domain to answer over HTTPS ("Getting a certificate for panel.example.com…"), then offers **Open panel.example.com**, which mints a one-time sign-in link to the new address for that person alone, as `dockwell recover` prints one (15 minutes, once), with a fresh passkey step-up, and lands on Profile and security with **Add a passkey here** (#445). The link is minted then, not at the save, so a certificate that takes longer than 15 minutes, as DNS and ACME's back-off can, doesn't leave it expired. Like `dockwell recover`'s, it carries its token in the fragment (`/recover#…`), so no server log or Referer holds it, and the page clears it from the address bar once used; Activity's `sign_in_link.create` names the link, never the token.

**From loopback to a domain,** the tunnel keeps working throughout, with the passkeys made there, and the person who set the domain is on it: that is the whole move.

**From one domain to another, the old one keeps answering.** Moving from A to B restarts the control plane on both names: B is the panel's domain, and A answers beside it, with its certificate, for a grace period. So:
- the page the person saved on, on A, keeps polling "Getting a certificate for B…", and when B fails, **Check again** and **Stop using it** are there on A, not only through the tunnel;
- everyone else keeps signing in on A with A's passkeys, which are bound to A's relying party ID, and each finds **This panel is moving to B** with **Move my sign-in to B**: a one-time link to B minted for them, after a fresh step-up on A, which lands on B's **Add a passkey here**;
- the confirm before the save names how many people have passkeys only for A, and Settings → Members shows who hasn't moved yet, with **Send a sign-in link** for each, for the owner to pass on, as an invite;
- A also serves `/.well-known/webauthn` listing B (WebAuthn's related origin requests). B's own ceremonies ask for B's relying party ID, so during the grace period B's sign-in page also offers **Use a passkey from A**, a ceremony that asks for A's ID, in a browser whose `getClientCapabilities()` says it supports related origins; elsewhere that button isn't shown, and Move my sign-in to B is the way.
- **Stop using it** during a move ends the move back on A: the setting goes back to A and the control plane restarts on A alone, so A's passkeys keep working. It never clears the setting while B isn't serving, which would leave every member without a way in.
- the tunnel writer of [ADR 0104](0104-adding-a-domain-publishes-it.md) keeps A as one of the panel's names for as long as it answers: whatever builds the move names A in `PanelNames.Moving`, so no rule Dockwell writes in a tunnel serves or moves it.

The grace period ends 7 days after B has answered over HTTPS and its owner has signed in there, or when the owner ends it early in Settings → General; then A answers 421 as any other name does. Loopback and the tunnel answer throughout. Moving back to a domain set before (A to B to A) brings A's passkeys back, as they're bound to A's ID.

**A domain from the setting never stops `dockwell run`.** Only a domain given on its command line or in its environment does, as today, since the operator who typed it is there to read why. When the setting is refused as `dockwell run` starts, as when a later release's rules refuse the name, `dockwell run` starts on loopback alone (and on A, during a move from A), with the tunnel, and the agent in the same process, logs why, and the panel shows the refusal on Settings → General with **Stop using it**. When the domain doesn't get a certificate, the control plane answers on loopback, and on the old domain during a move, as it does today with a domain whose certificate fails, and the panel shows the domain's state (no certificate yet, and why) with **Check again** and **Stop using it**.

**The way back is `dockwell panel-domain --clear`,** run on the control server with local access to the data directory, as `dockwell claim` and `dockwell recover` have, since a token can't call `SetPanelDomain`. It opens the store as they do: SQLite in the data directory, or Postgres from `DOCKWELL_DATABASE` in the shell, passed through sudo as [the install guide](../install.md) says for them (`sudo --preserve-env=DOCKWELL_DATABASE dockwell panel-domain --clear`), so it works while `dockwell run` is stopped or failing to start. It clears the setting (during a move it goes back to A, as Stop using it does, and run again it goes to loopback alone), goes on Activity, and restarts the service when it runs as root under systemd or OpenRC, or says the command that does. `dockwell recover` and the claim page name it beside themselves.

**Behind Caddy, the agent moves the panel's site as it starts.** On a control server with routes, Caddy serves the panel's domain (ADR 0024). As `dockwell run` starts with a new domain, its agent writes Caddy's panel site for it, and for the old one during a move, outside a plan, as it does today when `DOCKWELL_DOMAIN` changes between starts. That's no change to managed state: the routes are as they were, and the panel's site follows the setting, whose save the owner confirmed with a passkey and Activity holds.

## Consequences

- Settings → General's Panel address becomes a field with the checks beside it, Save and the restart it says, in place of the list of steps; the claim page says in a line that a domain can come later, under Settings (#440).
- `dockwell run` reads one more setting at start, after its flags and environment, and never stops for it. `docs/install.md` adds the panel as the first way to set the domain, and `dockwell panel-domain --clear` as the way back.
- Two methods (`CheckPanelDomain`, admin, and `SetPanelDomain`, owner) and a step-up action; `methods.ts` lists both, and the panel asks `may` for them (ADR 0059). One local command, `dockwell panel-domain`.
- For a move, the control plane serves two names for up to a week after the new one works, with a certificate for each, and passkey ceremonies take their relying party ID from the name the request came to, except **Use a passkey from A** on B, which asks for A's. Every member gets a way to B without SSH.
- A restart the panel asks for exists for the first time. Later settings that are read at start, such as the ACME directory, can use it the same way; until one does, they stay `dockwell run`'s.
- Without the domain set from the panel, nothing changes: an install that sets `DOCKWELL_DOMAIN` keeps working as it does.

## Amendment: behind a tunnel or a proxy that ends TLS

*2026-11-16, #734.*

The panel's domain may reach the control server through Cloudflare Tunnel, or another proxy that ends TLS: the browser is on `https://panel.example.com`, so passkeys and Secure cookies hold, but no certificate comes to this server, and nothing needs ports 80 or 443. The panel treated every domain as one it serves itself, so it said "Getting a certificate…" for ever.

- **A second choice for the panel's address.** Settings → General asks how the domain reaches the panel: **Dockwell serves it**, as above, or **Behind a tunnel or proxy that ends TLS**. The second is kept beside the domain in the store (`panel_domain_behind_proxy`), and `dockwell run` takes it from `--behind-proxy` (`DOCKWELL_BEHIND_PROXY`) with `--domain`. Behind a proxy, `dockwell run` asks for no certificate, holds no port 80 or 443 for the panel, and doesn't hand the domain to Caddy (ADR 0024). The panel keeps answering on loopback alone, and takes `https://<domain>` as its public origin: the relying party ID passkeys are made for, the origin their ceremonies check, `__Host-` cookies, and the links it makes.
- **Whom it believes.** A request is the domain's only where it comes over loopback, from the proxy on this server, as `cloudflared` on the host's network is, with `Host` set to the domain and `X-Forwarded-Proto: https`. The panel listens on loopback alone (ADR 0011), so loopback is the only proxy there can be; a proxy elsewhere reaches it through one on this server. Then, as Caddy's hand-over of the domain is (ADR 0024), the request reads as HTTPS on the domain, and the visitor's address is the last of `X-Forwarded-For`. Anything else that comes over loopback with those headers could already reach the panel there: it gets no more, as a passkey's assertion is bound to the browser's origin, and a cookie to the browser that holds it. From anyone else, neither header counts.
- **A live check that fits.** For a domain behind a proxy, Settings → General checks the way in, not DNS and ports: the control plane makes a one-time token, asks `https://<domain>/.well-known/dockwell-reach/<token>`, and sees it answered by this panel, through the proxy. It reads **Reached through your tunnel**, or why not, in a line with **Check again**: DNS with no tunnel route ("Cloudflare answered 530: the tunnel has no route for this name"), a tunnel pointing elsewhere ("502: the tunnel can't reach what it points at. Point it at http://127.0.0.1:7600"), or something else answering. The token answers once, within 30 seconds, and says nothing else.
- **Noticed for the person.** As the domain is typed, `CheckPanelDomain` says when its DNS leads to Cloudflare, by the ranges Cloudflare publishes; the panel then asks the way in, and offers **Behind a tunnel or proxy** where it reached the panel through a tunnel, so nobody waits for a certificate that can't come.
- **The docs.** The install guide's "Behind Cloudflare Tunnel or another proxy" gets a part for the panel, and its "the panel's own domain can't be served this way" goes.

Moving between the two choices on the same domain is a restart, as setting the domain is, and keeps every passkey, as the relying party ID doesn't change.

### The amendment, as built

- **The check's answers.** A 530 reads as a tunnel that isn't connected ("Start cloudflared on this server"), as that's what Cloudflare answers for a tunnel with no connector; a name the tunnel has no rule for gets cloudflared's own catch-all, which reads as something else answering. The token's answer is a second random value the request never carried, so a site that echoes its path doesn't pass. A token another Dockwell didn't make answers 404 with `Dockwell-Reach: unknown`, which reads as another Dockwell answering. Once the token comes, the check also says when the proxy reached the panel from beyond loopback, with another `Host`, as cloudflared's HTTP host header setting sends, or without `X-Forwarded-Proto: https`.
- **It asks through netguard,** with the private network and this server's own addresses allowed, as a proxy that ends TLS may be on either; the path is fixed, and nothing of the answer but its status and whether it matched comes back. Where the check connects to a private, loopback, link-local or carrier-grade NAT address, it answers reached or not and nothing more, so it doesn't tell an admin what answers on 443 inside (the review's L4). It's told by each address the client connects to, as it connects, not by a lookup of its own, which a name answering one address and then another could slip (the re-check's N3). A test binary asks only `DOCKWELL_TEST_REACH`, a stand-in for the tunnel, trusting `DOCKWELL_ACME_ROOTS`.
- **The token is answered on the loopback listener alone, whatever its host,** ahead of the guard, so a domain can be checked before it's saved; a request that came over TLS, or through Caddy's hand-over, isn't a tunnel's and goes on to the panel.
- **Cloudflare's proxy isn't its tunnel** (the review's M2). A record on Cloudflare's proxy without a tunnel leads to the same ranges, and its origin is this server's public address, where Dockwell serves the domain itself. So DNS alone chooses nothing: where it leads to Cloudflare, the way in is asked too, and the tunnel is chosen, or offered first, only where the check reached this panel through it. Cloudflare's 520 to 526 say it's the proxy connecting to this server's public address, where the answer carries Cloudflare's CF-RAY; from anything else a 52x is that origin's own (the re-check's N4), with both fixes: a tunnel to the loopback address, or Dockwell serving the domain under Full (strict).
- **A domain waiting for its certificate** asks `CheckPanelDomain` too, and the way in where it leads to Cloudflare: one reached through a tunnel reads "panel.example.com reaches this panel through a tunnel" with **Serve it behind a tunnel** first; any other keeps "Getting a certificate…" and offers it beside **Check again**.
- **Both ways on one domain** (the review's M3). A domain behind a tunnel offers **Serve it with its own certificate**, which shows the record to make and what holds a port first, as the field does, then the same confirm and passkey, as one change and one restart. Off the tunnel, the domain answers only once its record leads here and a certificate comes, so the confirm says so where the record doesn't lead here yet or the person is on the domain; while another program holds port 80 or 443 the restart waits, and the handler refuses it (`ports_held`), naming the program, as the restart would serve the domain nowhere. A domain that isn't served offers **Serve it behind a tunnel** beside **Stop using it**, first where the tunnel still reaches the panel, so going back is one restart too (the re-check's N1 and N2).
- **Activity** says "Served the panel on panel.example.com behind a tunnel or proxy" for a move between the two, from `EventSetting.behind_proxy` and `previous_behind_proxy`.
- **The tunnel's way in reuses the DNS record row,** as a "Tunnel" record of the domain and `http://127.0.0.1:7600`, ready to copy, with the check's state under it.
